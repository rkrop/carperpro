# Revised Visual & Art Direction — Auto Parts App

> Use this to **replace** the previous "automotive-industrial / dark graphite" style description in your design prompt. The functional spec (screens, flows, content in Mexican Spanish) stays the same. This only changes the look and feel.

---

## VISUAL DIRECTION (paste this into the design tool)

The brand promise is **Rápido, Simple y Eficiente**, and the visual design must *feel* like that — not just say it. The concept is **"precision and clarity":** the app should feel like a perfectly organized parts drawer or a well-designed engineering tool, calm and confident. Avoid the typical greasy, dark, industrial auto-parts aesthetic entirely.

**Base & atmosphere**
- Light, airy, and spacious. Use a warm off-white / near-white background with generous whitespace. The screen should feel calm and uncluttered so the part the user is looking for is the clear focus.
- Most of the interface is neutral and quiet; color appears only where it carries meaning (a primary action, a status, a key piece of information).

**Color**
- A near-neutral palette (warm whites, soft light grays, a deep near-black for text) plus **one single confident accent color** used sparingly — only on primary buttons and the most important highlights.
- Recommended accent: a strong, refined **electric/cobalt blue** (precise, trustworthy, modern) OR a clean **amber/orange** (energetic but not aggressive). Pick one and commit. Do NOT use aggressive automotive red.
- Use a calm green only as a status signal for "En existencia" / compatibility confirmation. Color should always mean something.

**Typography (this is the hero of the design)**
- A clean, modern geometric/grotesk sans-serif for the interface (e.g., Inter, Geist, or Space Grotesk). Use strong weight contrast: large, bold, confident headings and calm, legible body text.
- **Signature detail:** display all part numbers, SKUs, and OEM codes in a monospace font. This makes the data feel precise and engineered, reads as "technical and trustworthy," and gives the app a distinctive identity that competitors won't have.

**Layout & structure**
- Strict, consistent grid and a clean spacing scale (4/8px rhythm). "Ordered" means everything is aligned and breathing — no cramming.
- Crisp white cards with subtle borders or very soft shadows; small corner radius (precise, not bubbly/playful).
- Thin-to-medium line icons, consistent set, minimal.

**Product imagery**
- Clean, consistent product photos on plain neutral backgrounds (catalog-style), so the parts catalog feels premium and well-organized — the opposite of a cluttered online marketplace.

**Signature element**
- Make the compatibility badge a recognizable, well-designed component: a calm, confident "✓ Compatible con tu [vehículo]" treatment. It's the visual embodiment of the trust promise and should look intentional, not like a generic tag.

**Motion & feel**
- Everything should feel instant and effortless. Minimal, subtle skeleton loaders (no heavy splash screens or spinners). Fast, light, no friction.

## DO NOT (explicit guardrails)
- No dark graphite/black industrial theme, no carbon-fiber or metal textures, no "workshop / grease" mood.
- No aggressive red, no loud gradients, no heavy drop shadows on everything.
- No cluttered marketplace look, no rainbow of colors, no generic "rounded-everything tech app" template.
- Minimalist must not mean empty or boring — keep it confident, intentional, and well-crafted.

---

## Why this direction (rationale for you)

- **It differentiates you.** Every competitor leans dark/industrial. Going clean and light is instantly recognizable as "the modern one."
- **It reinforces the promise.** Whitespace and order *are* the feeling of "simple and efficient." The design proves the claim instead of just stating it.
- **It stays distinctive, not boring.** The monospace part numbers, the single confident accent, and the signature compatibility badge give it character without clutter.
- **It ages well.** Clean and typographic designs stay current far longer than trend-driven industrial themes.
